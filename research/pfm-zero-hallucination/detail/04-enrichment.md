# Transaction Enrichment for a Personal-Finance Assistant: Build vs Buy (as of July 2026)

## 1. SOTA accuracy for bank-transaction categorization — **VERIFIED (academic), PARTIAL (production claims)**

Peer-reviewed results on real bank data cluster in a consistent band. A transformer-based hierarchical classifier with taxonomy-aware attention ([arXiv 2312.07730](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.07730v1), Dec 2023) reports **macro-category F1 of 93.0% (card) / 95.1% (current account)** but only **84.5% / 86.7% F1 at the fine-grained (micro-category) level** — a good proxy for what a well-built in-house system achieves: ~90%+ on a coarse taxonomy (~15–25 categories), dropping ~8–10 points at 100+ categories. Weakly-supervised approaches ([arXiv 2305.18430](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18430)) and synthetic-data SME work ([arXiv 2508.05425](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.05425), Aug 2025) confirm labeled-data scarcity is the binding constraint, not model architecture. Industry sources caution that benchmarks are biased toward clean, well-known merchants; the long tail of messy descriptors is where production accuracy is lost ([Triqai, 2025](https://www.triqai.com/article/automated-transaction-categorization-best-practices)). Vendor claims (below) of 95–99% are self-published and typically measured on their own taxonomies/datasets — treat as PARTIAL.

**Realistic planning numbers:** 85–92% accuracy on a 15–25-category consumer taxonomy with good merchant enrichment; 75–87% F1 at 100+ categories. Taxonomies much beyond ~120 detailed categories are rare in production (Plaid: 104 detailed; MX: 119).

## 2. MCC reliability — **VERIFIED that MCC is unreliable; PARTIAL on region-level rates**

This directly pressure-tests Doc A. The strongest quantified figure comes from Tapix (a vendor, but with data across 1B+ monthly European transactions): **only ~63% of transactions were correctly categorized by MCC alone — roughly one in three lands in the wrong category or none** ([Tapix: "What are MCC codes and why banks need more"](https://www.tapix.io/resources/post/why-mcc-codes-do-not-help-much-with-payment-categorization)). Documented failure modes: merchants keep the MCC assigned at onboarding as their business evolves; the same chain carries different MCCs across locations/acquirers; networks assign inconsistent codes to the same merchant ([Ramp MCC guide](https://ramp.com/blog/merchant-category-code-list); [Bill.com](https://www.bill.com/learning/merchant-category-codes); [Stax](https://staxpayments.com/blog/merchant-category-codes/)). Deliberate miscoding to dodge interchange tiers is common enough that acquirer assessments for miscoding have hit seven figures ([Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/magazine_articles/miscoded-merchants-can-be-a-seven-figure-mistake/)). Additionally, **MCC simply doesn't exist for non-card rails** (ACH/bank transfers, mobile money, instant payments) — a critical gap for African markets where wallet and transfer rails dominate. **NOT FOUND:** peer-reviewed region-by-region or acquirer-level miscoding rates; no Visa/Mastercard-published error statistics. **Recommendation: demote MCC in Doc A from primary signal to a weak prior feature.**

## 4. Open taxonomies — **VERIFIED**

Plaid publishes its full Personal Finance Category taxonomy as a free CSV: **16 primary / 104 detailed categories**, now PFCv2 ([taxonomy CSV](https://plaid.com/documents/transactions-personal-finance-category-taxonomy.csv), [PFCv2 CSV](https://plaid.com/documents/pfc-taxonomy-all.csv), [announcement](https://plaid.com/blog/transactions-categorization-taxonomy/)). This is the de-facto open standard and a sensible starting taxonomy even if you build. MX's 119 categories and Ntropy's consumer hierarchy (API endpoint) are documented but not open-licensed in the same way.

## 5. Fact-check D5 (small fine-tuned encoders vs LLMs) — **VERIFIED, with a caveat**

D5 holds up in 2026. A Feb 2026 production study ([arXiv 2602.06370](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06370v1)) finds fine-tuned BERT-family encoders achieve competitive-to-superior classification at **1–2 orders of magnitude lower cost and latency** than zero/few-shot LLM prompting. A transaction-specific comparison found a specialized model beating the best LLM tested with **92% fewer parameters and ~300× faster inference (0.27ms vs 84.9ms)** ([arXiv 2502.15724](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.15724)). Ntropy's own benchmark showed its pipeline beating GPT-4 by 15 points on label accuracy at ~100× lower latency, and a fine-tuned Llama beating GPT-4 by ~7 points ([Ntropy repo](https://github.com/ntropy-network/enrichment_models)). **Caveat:** LLMs win on the long tail and zero-shot novel merchants — Ntropy itself uses LLMs plus aggressive caching to amortize cost ([caching blog](https://www.ntropy.com/blog/caching-framework)). The 2026 consensus pattern: **cached lookup → fine-tuned encoder for the head → LLM fallback for the unseen tail.**

## 6. Merchant name normalization SOTA — **VERIFIED (approaches), PARTIAL (end-to-end published systems)**

State of the art is embedding-based entity resolution over normalized strings: fine-tuned sentence transformers for merchant matching ([Fetch engineering blog](https://techblog.fetch.com/optimizing-sentence-transformers-for-entity-resolution-fb07be78e5e5)); embed-then-cluster with cosine similarity thresholds (~0.93) and greedy agglomeration ([arXiv 2509.09544](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.09544)); character-level transformer encoders for raw descriptor parsing (Mastercard/Visa patents, e.g. [US 11989165](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11989165)); feature-extraction + clustering for counterparty normalization ([arXiv 2508.21081](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21081)); small LMs for zero-shot entity matching ([AnyMatch, arXiv 2409.04073](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.04073)). The hard part is not the model — it's the **curated merchant database** (Ntropy: 100M+ entities; Tapix: 800K merchants), which is the true vendor moat.

## Recommendation: **Buy for card-rail head coverage where a vendor exists; build the Africa-critical path**

Evidence trail: (a) vendors demonstrably beat generic in-house/LLM baselines on head merchants — Ntropy's benchmark vs GPT-4, MX's +30pt case study — because the moat is merchant databases, not models; (b) but **no major vendor covers Africa** — Plaid Enrich and Spade are US/CA-only, Tapix/Snowdrop are EMEA/APAC, and the African open-finance layer is thin and volatile (Okra's May 2025 shutdown); (c) MCC is unusable as a primary signal (~63% correct, absent entirely on transfer/mobile-money rails); (d) fine-tuned encoders + LLM tail fallback make a credible in-house build cheap to run (sub-ms inference) with realistic 85–90% accuracy on a Plaid-style 16-primary taxonomy. **Concretely:** adopt Plaid's open PFC taxonomy; if the product serves US/EU users, buy (Ntropy or Tapix for multi-region, Plaid/Spade if US-only); for African rails, build — a cached merchant dictionary + fine-tuned encoder + LLM fallback — since no purchasable alternative exists, and revise Doc A to treat MCC as a weak prior, not a primary signal.
