# Ledger & Data-Foundation Patterns for a PFM Product — Research Report (as of 2026-07-26)

## 1. Bi-temporal ledger design; double-entry vs single-entry — **VERIFIED (design guidance); PARTIAL (PFM norms)**

**Modern Treasury** has the most complete public corpus: the "How to Scale a Ledger" series (Parts I–VI) plus [Enforcing Immutability in your Double-Entry Ledger](https://www.moderntreasury.com/journal/enforcing-immutability-in-your-double-entry-ledger). Core principles: append-only (mistakes fixed by new correcting entries, never overwrites), double-entry enforced at write time, and three core objects (account / entry / transaction). See [Part V: Immutability and Double-Entry](https://www.moderntreasury.com/journal/how-to-scale-a-ledger-part-v) and [Part VI: Concurrency Controls](https://www.moderntreasury.com/journal/how-to-scale-a-ledger-part-vi). Their bi-temporal answer is explicit: ledger transactions carry `effective_at` (when the money movement really happened, supports backdated settlement) separate from `posted_at` (when recorded), with all entries inheriting the transaction's `effective_at` — documented in [Timing Fields in Linked Transactions](https://docs.moderntreasury.com/ledgers/docs/timing-fields-in-linked-transactions) (accessed 2026-07-26). 2025 updates: multi-region Ledgers (June 2025), dashboard ledger-account updates ([2.3.2025 release notes](https://docs.moderntreasury.com/platform/changelog/release-notes-2-3-2025)), and "Floats Don't Work For Storing Cents" (Aug 6, 2025) — use integer minor units.

**TigerBeetle** publishes its rationale in [Debit/Credit: The Schema for OLTP](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/concepts/debit-credit/) and [Financial Accounting](https://docs.tigerbeetle.com/coding/financial-accounting/): fixed-size immutable accounts/transfers, double-entry invariants enforced in the database, ~1M TPS design goal. 2025–2026 updates: a [Jepsen analysis of 0.16.11](https://jepsen.io/analyses/tigerbeetle-0.16.11) (2025), a $24M Series A ([FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/data-privacy-security/financial-transactions-database-tigerbeetle-raises-24m-series-a-funding)), active releases through July 2026 ([GitHub releases](https://github.com/tigerbeetle/tigerbeetle/releases)).

**Is double-entry the norm for PFM/aggregation?** No — and this is the key nuance. Both MT and TigerBeetle are *money-movement* systems (they are the system of record). PFM products mirror external systems of record, and published open-source practice splits: **Maybe** (Rails, open source) uses a single-entry polymorphic `Entry` model with event-sourced-style immutable entries materialized into balance caches ([openalternative.co/maybe](https://openalternative.co/maybe), accessed 2026-07-26), while **Firefly III** uses double-entry. No authoritative survey exists → PARTIAL. The practical consensus: double-entry buys you nothing when you don't control both legs; single-entry observations + derived balances is the dominant PFM pattern.

## 2. Pending→posted reconciliation — **PARTIAL** (vendor behavior documented; matching internals mostly proprietary)

**Plaid** documents the contract in [Transaction states](https://plaid.com/docs/transactions/transactions-data/): a posted transaction is a *new* transaction carrying `pending_transaction_id` pointing at the removed pending one; name and amount may differ (e.g., tip added); and "in some rare cases" matching fails and the field is absent — apps must handle both versions themselves ([Troubleshooting](https://plaid.com/docs/transactions/troubleshooting/)). Plaid's engineering blog [Finding the Right Fit](https://plaid.com/blog/finding-the-right-fit-how-plaid-reconciles-pending-and-posted-transactions/) (2019; discussed on [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20060921), May 2019) describes a Random-Forest ML matcher that was "effective but not excellent" with a high false-negative rate. (Direct fetch of both Plaid pages returned 403 bot-blocks; content confirmed via search excerpts and HN — hence PARTIAL.) Finicity/Mastercard documents ["Pending and Shadow Transactions"](https://docs.finicity.com/pending-and-shadow-transactions/) — same pattern of pending records replaced by posted ones (403 on fetch; PARTIAL).

**Is there standard published matching logic? NOT FOUND.** No cross-industry standard exists. Aggregators expose best-effort IDs; everyone downstream rolls their own fallback matcher (typical published heuristics: same account, amount within tolerance, date within a few days, fuzzy name match — see patents like [US10885500](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10885500)). Plan for: amount drift (tips, FX), 1 pending → 1 posted only (multi-pending merges are unmatched), and pendings that simply expire.

## 3. Transfer-pair detection — **PARTIAL** (heuristics published; accuracy rates NOT FOUND)

- **Published heuristic** (Finary help center, [Internal transfers: automatic detection](https://help.finary.com/en/articles/11572132-internal-transfers-automatic-detection-and-exclusion-from-analysis), accessed 2026-07-26 via search excerpt): opposite amounts (+50/−50), dates within ±5 days, pair the closest-dated candidates; user override supported. Patents ([US11188981](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11188981)) cover the same amount/date-window pairing.
- **Plaid**: PFC taxonomy has `TRANSFER_IN`/`TRANSFER_OUT` categories ([taxonomy CSV](https://plaid.com/documents/transactions-personal-finance-category-taxonomy.csv); PFCv2 with AI-enhanced categorization released December 2025 — [Plaid blog](https://plaid.com/blog/ai-enhanced-transaction-categorization/)), and [/transactions/recurring/get](https://plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions/) detects recurring streams (≥3 occurrences, plus `early_detection`). But Plaid labels *single legs* as transfers; it does not publish a cross-account pair-linking feature.
- **Ntropy**: consumer taxonomy includes explicit `inter account transfer`, `intra account transfer`, `transfer from investment app`, `peer to peer transfer` labels ([Ntropy Categories docs](https://docs.ntropy.com/enrichment/categories), accessed 2026-07-26) — again leg-level labels, not pairing.
- **Accuracy rates: NOT FOUND.** No vendor publishes precision/recall for pair-linking. Conclusion: leg classification is buyable; the pairing join is roll-your-own.

## 4. DuckDB per-tenant / embedded analytics at scale — **VERIFIED** (patterns and limits are well documented)

Production write-ups from 2024–2026 converge on three patterns (e.g., [FlowVerify: DuckDB in SaaS multi-tenant analytics](https://www.flowverify.co/blog/duckdb-saas-analytics-multitenant-2026), [multi-tenant catalogs on object storage](https://medium.com/@komalbaparmar007/duckdb-multi-tenant-catalogs-on-object-storage-portable-analytics-for-every-customer-c16981d034fc), [endjin's DuckDB in Practice](https://endjin.com/blog/2025/04/duckdb-in-practice-enterprise-integration-architectural-patterns), April 2025): (A) one `.duckdb` file per tenant — clean isolation, backup = file copy, but machine-pinning and file-handle pressure at thousands of active tenants; (B) shared instance with per-tenant schemas — no row-level security, one missed tenant filter leaks data; (C) tiny per-tenant DuckDB catalog + Parquet on object storage — the most operable at scale.

**Where it breaks:** single-writer concurrency (one write transaction; multiple web processes can't share a file for writes — [DuckDB concurrency docs](https://duckdb.org/docs/current/connect/concurrency), [discussion #22288](https://github.com/duckdb/duckdb/discussions/22288)); aggressive RAM use (set `memory_limit` below container limits or get OOM-killed); degradation on much-larger-than-memory data ([Running DuckDB in Production](https://www.dench.com/blog/duckdb-in-production)).

**2026 calculus change — yes, materially.** [DuckLake v1.0 shipped April 13, 2026](https://ducklake.select/2026/04/13/ducklake-10/) (production-ready, backward-compatible; DuckDB 1.5.2), putting lakehouse metadata in a SQL catalog (Postgres/SQLite/DuckDB) and enabling multi-writer ACID over Parquet — directly addressing the single-writer problem. And [MotherDuck's "hypertenancy"](https://motherduck.com/product/hypertenancy/) provisions a dedicated DuckDB "duckling" per user that scales to zero, with 2025–2026 [case studies](https://motherduck.com/case-studies/) (Layers, Together AI). DuckDB-per-tenant is production-credible in 2026 *if* you adopt pattern C or MotherDuck rather than raw per-tenant files on app servers.

## 5. Append-only + corrections vs update-in-place — **VERIFIED**

Best-practice writing is consistent: the [Azure Event Sourcing pattern](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/event-sourcing) (Microsoft, current) — event store is never updated; corrections are *compensating events*; Modern Treasury's immutability posts (§1) say the same in ledger terms; Maybe's architecture applies it to PFM (immutable entries + materialized balance caches). Caveats from practitioner writing ([Event Sourcing 101](https://dzone.com/articles/event-sourcing-guide-when-to-use-avoid-pitfalls), 2025): full event sourcing adds replay/versioning complexity — for financial data the load-bearing property is *append-only with correction records and derived read models*, not a full CQRS framework.

## Recommendations for the PFM data foundation

1. **Ledger model:** single-entry, append-only observation ledger (Maybe-style `Entry`), not double-entry — you mirror external systems of record. Store integer minor units. Make it bi-temporal: `effective_at` (bank's transaction date) + `recorded_at` (ingestion time), per Modern Treasury's pattern. Reserve double-entry (or TigerBeetle) only if you later move money.
2. **Corrections:** never update-in-place. New superseding/correction records with a pointer to the superseded row; materialize current-state and balance tables asynchronously.
3. **Pending→posted:** trust `pending_transaction_id` when present; always run your own fallback matcher (same account, amount tolerance for tips/FX, ±5-day window, fuzzy name) because Plaid documents that matching sometimes fails. Keep both versions in the immutable store, link them, surface one.
4. **Transfer pairs:** buy leg-level labels (Plaid PFCv2 `TRANSFER_IN/OUT` or Ntropy inter-account-transfer), then do your own pairing join: opposite amounts, ±3–5 days, cross-account, with user override. No vendor sells the pairing; no published accuracy benchmarks exist.
5. **DuckDB-per-tenant: credible, with structure.** For per-user PFM analytics workloads (small data, bursty reads) use per-tenant DuckDB catalogs + Parquet on object storage or MotherDuck hypertenancy; adopt DuckLake 1.0 (prod-ready since 2026-04) if you need concurrent writers. Keep Postgres as the transactional system of record; DuckDB is the analytics layer, not the ledger.
